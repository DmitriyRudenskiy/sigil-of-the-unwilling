# Campaign archetype profiles (task 2.1)

## Authority and adaptation boundary

- The seven names and ancestry/group boundary come from the canonical campaign roster in [`docs/02e3-races.md` §9.4](../../../docs/02e3-races.md) and the campaign population spec. Archetype mechanics apply by group; ancestry remains identity metadata.
- The source comparison uses pinned [`FrankRuis/ats_cheatsheet` `data/Species.csv`](https://github.com/FrankRuis/ats_cheatsheet/blob/cea861b4a6a3668685f1b727def6d7d66565a5f8/data/Species.csv) at commit `cea861b4a6a3668685f1b727def6d7d66565a5f8` (2026-08-05). It describes seven species' need inputs and work affinities. The Fextralife Species pages cited in [`source-inventory.md`](source-inventory.md) are older qualitative corroboration for only five species.
- This is an adaptation, not a second species/Resolve system. Do **not** import source-game Resolve, reputation, decadence, hunger tolerance, species housing, goods, storm cycle, or chance-based yield rolls. Source needs identify patterns only. The campaign MVP has one archetype-specific need per group; basic food consumption remains a shared campaign rule and is not charged a second time as a second group need.
- Existing project source `docs/02g-city-hex.md` §5.1 supplies the qualitative response tiers (low/medium/high) for the seven campaign groups. Its score, Resolve, storm penalties, and other standalone score-mode formulas are not carried over. Tiers are translated below to a small deterministic campaign rule.

`education`, `brawling`, `treatment`, `religion`, and `market_access` are **service/capacity IDs**, not resource IDs or spendable items. Their provider buildings and capacities are resolved in the unified building catalog. `food` is the sole signature need that directly observes a registered MVP resource; the satisfaction check reads the existing population food flow and must not consume food a second time. If a service provider has recipe inputs, those inputs must independently resolve to registered resources under task 1.4.

## Deterministic parameter contract

| Parameter | Gameplay explanation | Resolution rule |
|---|---|---|
| `signature_need` | Gives each group one legible reason to care about a city service or resource without copying the many-item AtS shopping list. | Need demand is the number of residents in the group. For a service, compare that demand with active service capacity; aggregate the populations of groups sharing a need ID before checking coverage, so capacity cannot be counted twice. For `food`, compare the existing global food supply/consumption result with total population food demand and expose that same coverage to the Farmers & Brewers group; do not allocate or consume the stock a second time. Coverage is `100` when demand is zero; otherwise `floor(100 * min(demand, supplied) / demand)`. No service capacity means zero coverage. |
| `preferred_roles` | Uses AtS-style work affinities to make population composition matter without forbidding jobs. | Any otherwise-eligible resident may fill a job. Count assigned workers whose archetype and building role match; they receive the declared deterministic specialization modifier. A mismatch is never a hard job lock. The modifier is shown in the production breakdown; the production transaction still uses only the building's registered recipe. |
| `specialization_bonus_bp` | Converts source-game specialization/bonus-yield inspiration into a predictable campaign output effect. | Start at `1000` basis points (+10%) per matched worker, bounded by the building's available job slots and a `10000` basis-point (+100%) cap. Apply once to that building's eligible output using deterministic fixed-point arithmetic: for each building/recipe/output key, `scaled = base_output * (10000 + bonus_bp) + saved_remainder`; emit `floor(scaled / 10000)` whole units and save `scaled % 10000` as the next remainder. No random double-yield roll. Remainders are state, not stock, and reset only when the keyed recipe/output is removed. Conservation tests belong to tasks 2.4/3.3. |
| `response_tier` | Models that groups differ in how quickly repeated unmet needs affect satisfaction, without using AtS Resolve thresholds. | Map low/medium/high to an unmet-response period of 1/2/3 consecutive campaign turns. When coverage is 100%, increase that group's satisfaction by 1 (cap 100) and reset its unmet counter. Otherwise increment the counter; each time it reaches a multiple of the group's period, reduce satisfaction by 1 (floor 0). Partial coverage is displayed but counts as unmet for this deliberately small MVP rule. |
| `initial_satisfaction` | Supplies a neutral state for a newly created group without copying source-game Base Resolve. | Campaign scenario data sets it explicitly; absent a scenario override, initialize to 50 on a 0–100 scale. This is a campaign default, not an AtS statistic. |

All arithmetic is integer/fixed-point and ordered by the campaign turn processor. No random roll, AtS goods, Amber, Resolve, reputation, or implicit race/job exclusion participates. Satisfaction remains distinct from Sign Prestige and from unit/party morale. The building catalog and later turn integration must expose each need's coverage, response counter, and modifier cause in the ledger/UI.

## Completion audit

- Every archetype has a stable ID, canonical group name, AtS comparison, need examples, one campaign signature need, preferred roles, response tier, and an explicit resolution rule.
- Each AtS mismatch is labeled as a campaign adaptation rather than misrepresented as a source-game need.
- Every rule uses campaign population, registered food, building capacity, or declared fixed-point modifiers; source-only items are examples only and cannot enter transactions.
- No parameter adds a city level, source-game victory condition, new currency, or separate unit roster.
